How cutting works & limitations

Every object is real geometry, and it changes shape while you drag. The knife carves a thin slot that follows your finger, and a piece falls away the moment the slot fully crosses it. The peeler removes a strip under the blade, and that strip becomes the curl, peel or chunk you see. Nothing is a canned animation.

The blade sits in the cut it has made. If you start on top of an object, it presses in as it travels. If you come in from the side, it slides in at board level. Harder materials make the blade lag a little behind your finger.

Use Tilt for angled cuts, Lengthwise or Across for perfectly straight cuts through the whole piece, and Width and Thickness to size the peeler. Follow view with a low camera cuts horizontally.

Squish: press and hold to dent an object. Clay, sand and dough keep the dent, jelly and stress balls spring back, and chocolate, ice and soap crack into real pieces. Dicer: sweep across an object to turn it into hundreds of cubes in the colours of its inside.

Each material reacts in its own way. Soap and wax curl, chocolate curls break off, meat drapes, fruit and vegetables peel, jelly and clay come away in wobbly chunks, and sand, bread and cake crumble. Soft materials wobble visually but are solid pieces, not true soft-body simulations.

Vibration works on Android browsers; iPhone Safari offers limited haptics. On iPhone, turn off silent mode to hear sound.
